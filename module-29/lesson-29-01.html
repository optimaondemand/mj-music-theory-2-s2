📚 KEY VOCABULARY
Neapolitan Chord
A major triad built on the lowered second scale degree (♭II); typically appears in first inversion (N6) in minor keys as a predominant chord. (OMT p. 490)
Augmented Sixth Chord
A chromatic chord containing an augmented 6th interval between the bass and a chromatic upper voice; the augmented 6th resolves outward by half step to the octave (dominant pitch). (OMT p. 496)
Italian (It+6)
♭6–1–♯4 above the bass. Three notes. Thin, open sound.
French (Fr+6)
♭6–1–2–♯4. Four notes. Adds scale degree 2. Whole-tone color.
German (Ger+6)
♭6–1–♭3–♯4. Four notes. Rich; enharmonic to a dominant 7th.
🎶 PART 1 · THE NEAPOLITAN CHORD

The Neapolitan chord (♭II) is a chromatic predominant: it intensifies the approach to the dominant from the flat side rather than from within the key. In A minor, the diatonic second scale degree is B. Flatten it to B♭, build a major triad on it (B♭–D–F), and put that triad in first inversion so that D is in the bass. That is the N6. It then resolves to V (E major), often directly or through a cadential I6/4. The ♭2 in the bass moves down by step to the leading tone (G♯) or up to scale degree 5 (E).

How to build N6 in any minor key: (1) Find scale degree 2. (2) Lower it by a half step to get ♭2. (3) Build a major triad on ♭2. (4) Put the triad in first inversion (♭2 is the bass note). You now have an N6.

💡 KEY POINT
The N6 label = first inversion. The N stands for Neapolitan, which is a chromatic supertonic major triad that approaches the dominant from a half step above.
🎸 PART 2 · AUGMENTED SIXTH CHORDS

All three augmented sixth chords share the same outer interval: ♭6 in the bass and ♯4 in an upper voice, spanning an augmented sixth. That interval resolves outward: ♭6 steps DOWN by half step to 5 (the dominant pitch), and ♯4 steps UP by half step to 5. Both chromatic voices converge on the same pitch: the dominant. The three types differ only in their inner notes.

💡 KEY POINT
The Ger+6 = F–A–C–D♯ in A minor. Respell D♯ as E♭ and you get F–A–C–E♭ = an F dominant seventh chord. Composers use this enharmonic equivalence to pivot to distant keys.

🤖 WORTH KNOWING · AI AND CHROMATIC HARMONY
AI harmonic-analysis tools can mislabel rarer chromatic chords like the Neapolitan or the augmented sixths, sometimes reading them as more ordinary diatonic chords. The resolution rules covered in this lesson, N6 to V, augmented sixth outward to the dominant pitch, are exactly what let a student check a label like that, whether it came from their own work or from a tool. Knowing how these chords resolve is what makes the label trustworthy, not the label itself.

💪 THE ONE THING TO REMEMBER

The Neapolitan (N6) is a major triad on ♭2 in first inversion, resolving to V as a chromatic predominant. The three augmented sixth chords (It+6, Fr+6, Ger+6) share the outer interval of ♭6 to ♯4, which resolves outward by half step to the dominant pitch. The Ger+6 is enharmonically a dominant seventh chord, making it a pivot for chromatic modulation.
