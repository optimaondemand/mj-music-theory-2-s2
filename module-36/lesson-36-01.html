🎵 TODAY'S FOCUS
How do all the parts of Music Theory II fit together into a single integrated understanding of how music works?
You have spent this year building layer by layer: minor scales, modes, counterpoint, form, jazz, world music, chromatic harmony, and composition. This lesson maps the whole arc, shows how each skill connects to the others, and traces how Theory II deepens every foundation from Theory I. The Q4 Capstone Assessment will ask you to draw on this whole picture at once.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to trace how Theory I concepts (major scales, triads, basic cadences) were deepened in Theory II.
2
I will be able to connect the four quarters of Theory II into a single map of skills.
3
I will be able to apply chromatic harmony concepts (Neapolitan, augmented sixths, modulation) in analysis and articulate how they connect to earlier material.

💡 KEY POINT
You did not "replace" Theory I. Every Rule from Theory I still applies; Theory II added exceptions, extensions, and new territories. 🎹 PART 3 · Q4 CHROMATIC HARMONY AT A GLANCE

The Capstone Assessment focuses especially on Q4 chromatic harmony. Here is a precise summary of the three key chord types you need to identify and label.

NEAPOLITAN (♭II / N6)
Built on the flatted second scale degree; major triad; almost always in first inversion (N6). Functions as a pre-dominant (like ii° or IV), moving to V.
In C minor:
♭II = D♭ major triad (D♭-F-A♭)
N6 = first inversion: F in bass
Resolves: N6 → V → i
AUGMENTED SIXTHS (It+6, Fr+6, Ger+6)
Built on ♭6 with an augmented sixth interval (♭6 to ♯4). All three expand outward to V; differ in inner voices.
Italian (It+6): ♭6, 1, ♯4
French (Fr+6): ♭6, 1, 2, ♯4
German (Ger+6): ♭6, 1, ♭3, ♯4
All resolve: Aug.6th → V
MODULATION (PIVOT CHORD)
The pivot chord belongs to both keys and is labeled with dual Roman numerals. The pivot makes the modulation smooth; the new key is confirmed by a cadence.
C major to G major:
Pivot = C-E-G
Old key: I in C
New key: IV in G
Confirmed by V-I in G
📌 WHAT THE CAPSTONE WILL ASK
Part A of the Capstone gives you a 20-24 measure tonal passage. You will: (1) assign Roman numerals to every chord, (2) identify any Neapolitan or augmented sixth chords, (3) label the modulation with dual Roman numerals, (4) name all cadence types, and (5) identify the form. Work carefully: every measure has a function, and function labels must match what you hear and see on the page.

🤖 WORTH KNOWING · AI AND MUSIC ANALYSIS
AI tools could organize study notes or even attempt a harmonic analysis of a tonal passage. The Capstone Assessment asks for more: your own analysis, your own composition re-exported as audio, and a recorded walkthrough where you explain your choices out loud. Work that was generated rather than reasoned through would not hold up under that kind of discussion, since the walkthrough asks you to explain your thinking, not just present a result.

💪 THE ONE THING TO REMEMBER

Theory II deepened everything from Theory I: major became major and minor, triads became seventh chords, diatonic became chromatic, one tradition became many. The Neapolitan and augmented sixth chords are the furthest extensions of that chromatic reach. Every skill you built is still active.
