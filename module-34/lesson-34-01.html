🎶 PART 1 · THE 12-BAR BLUES FORM

The 12-bar blues is one of the most recognizable forms in Western music. Every bar carries a specific chord. Three dominant seventh chords do all the work: I7 (home), IV7 (subdominant), and V7 (dominant). Dominant sevenths (major triad + minor seventh) give the blues its characteristic gritty tension even on the home chord.

The 12-Bar Blues Form Grid (example key: G)
Bars 1-4: I7. Bars 5-6: IV7. Bars 7-8: I7. Bar 9: V7. Bar 10: IV7. Bars 11-12: I7 then V7 (turnaround back to bar 1).

The turnaround (bar 12, V7) pulls the ear back to the start. After the turnaround, bar 1 begins again. Blues can loop this form endlessly for improvisation or stop at any chorus for a composed ending.

💡 KEY POINT
All three chords are dominant sevenths (major triad + minor 7th). In functional harmony, only V7 is supposed to be a dominant seventh. Blues uses I7 and IV7 as dominant sevenths too, which gives the entire form a characteristic restless tension even at rest on the tonic.
🎸 PART 2 · THE AAB MELODY SHAPE

Blues melodies almost always follow an AAB phrase pattern even when there are no lyrics. Phrase A (the call) states a musical idea over bars 1-4 (I7). Phrase A' (a variation or repetition) restates the idea over bars 5-8 as the harmony moves to IV7 and back. Phrase B (the response) answers over bars 9-12 as the V7-IV7-I7-V7 sequence closes the chorus.

AAB Phrase Shape Mapped Over the 12-Bar Form

The A phrase lands on the tonic at the end of bar 4. The A' phrase shifts when the harmony moves to IV7 (bar 5), so the melody may need to adjust slightly even if the shape stays similar. The B phrase climbs higher in response (bars 9-10) and then falls to a firm close at bar 11, before the turnaround begins.

💡 COMPOSER INSIGHT
A common blues melody strategy: the A phrase ends on a held note or rest (the “call” leaves space). The B response fills that space with a busier phrase that drives to the cadence. That balance of statement and answer is the engine of blues expressiveness.
🎹 PART 3 · THE BLUES SCALE AND BLUE NOTES

The blues scale is a six-note scale: it takes a minor pentatonic scale and adds one note, the ♭5 (the “blue note”). Three scale degrees carry the blues color: ♭3, ♭5, and ♭7. These minor-inflected pitches played over major dominant harmonies create the blues sound.

The Blues Scale in G, Six Notes, Three Blue Notes
The blues scale = minor pentatonic + ♭5. Blue notes played over major dominant chords generate the characteristic blues tension.

Notice what makes blue notes special: they are minor intervals (♭3, ♭5, ♭7) played over major dominant seventh chords. That clash between minor melody and major harmony is the sound of the blues. Blues performers often bend blue notes (sliding up from just below the target pitch) or use vibrato (oscillating the pitch quickly), effects that standard notation cannot capture.

🎵 PERFORMANCE PRACTICE NOTE
You can compose your blues melody in MuseScore or on staff paper, but if you perform it, you are allowed (and encouraged) to add expressive nuances: bends, slides, vibrato. The notation is a map, not the whole territory. In your composer statement, describe the expressive effect you aimed for.

✍️ YOUR COMPOSITION TASK

Now you compose. Your task is to write an original 12-bar blues melody in any key you like. You are not recreating an existing song. You are building something that has never existed before.

COMPOSITION REQUIREMENTS
1
12-bar form with the correct I7-IV7-V7 arrangement. Your melody must cover all 12 bars.
2
AAB melody shape: an A phrase (call), an A' phrase (variation), and a B phrase (response).
3
Blue notes used expressively: use at least two of the three blue notes (♭3, ♭5, ♭7) somewhere in your melody.
4
Composer statement (3-5 sentences): describe your call-and-response structure, where you placed blue notes and why, and the expressive quality you aimed for.

Format: Submit your melody as a MuseScore file, a PDF of staff notation, or a recording (audio/video). Your composer statement goes in the text submission box. Both are required.

A playback from notation software counts as a “performance” if you cannot record live. Sing, play, or use software. You decide.

🤖 WORTH KNOWING · AI AND BLUES COMPOSITION
An AI tool could generate a 12-bar blues in moments, complete with a plausible melody and blue notes in the right places. This assignment requires something more specific: the student's own composing within the AAB blues form, performing or exporting the audio, and writing a genuine self-critique. Producing a finished-sounding track is not the point; composing and reflecting on your own choices is.

💪 THE ONE THING TO REMEMBER

Blues composition is built on three interlocking structures: the 12-bar I7-IV7-V7 form, the AAB call-and-response melody shape, and the blues scale (with its ♭3, ♭5, and ♭7 blue notes). The blues sound comes from the tension between minor-inflected melody and major dominant harmony. Your original melody is where those three structures become something personal.
