🎵 TODAY'S FOCUS
When a melody steps outside the scale, how do you name, hear, and write those chromatic pitches?
Diatonic melodies use only the notes of the key. Advanced melodies add chromatic pitches: notes a half step above or below a diatonic note. The moveable-Do solfege system has a syllable for every chromatic pitch. Once you know those syllables, you can name any note you hear, sing any note you see, and write down melodies that wander far from home.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the chromatic solfege syllable for every raised and lowered pitch in the moveable-Do system.
2
I will be able to apply a five-step strategy to sight-sing a melody containing chromatic pitches.
3
I will be able to write down a melody with chromatic pitches after listening (melodic dictation).

🎶 PART 1 · THE CHROMATIC SOLFEGE LADDER

Moveable-Do solfege names every pitch relative to Do (the tonic). For the seven diatonic degrees you already know: Do Re Mi Fa Sol La Ti Do. Chromatic pitches sit between those rungs. The rule is simple: raise a pitch by adding the vowel sound "ee" (Di, Ri, Fi, Si, Li); lower a pitch by changing the vowel to "e" or "a" (Ra, Me, Se, Le, Te).

💡 THE PATTERN
Raised syllables add an "ee" sound to the vowel: Di, Ri, Fi, Si, Li. Lowered syllables change the vowel: Ra, Me, Se, Le, Te. There is no "raised Mi" (Fi and Mi are enharmonically adjacent but named differently) and no "raised Ti" (that would be Do). There is also no "lowered Fa" (Me and Fa are a half step apart by definition).
🎸 PART 2 · FIVE-STEP SIGHT-SINGING STRATEGY

When you encounter a chromatic melody, use this five-step approach. Practice it in order every time until it becomes automatic. Inner hearing is the goal: the ability to hear the melody mentally before you open your mouth.

1
Establish the key
Read the key signature. Sing or hum Do (the tonic) to fix it in your inner ear before you begin.
2
Find Do
Locate the tonic pitch in the melody (or in your starting pitch). Every other pitch is measured from Do.
3
Scan for chromatic pitches
Look through the melody before you sing. Find every accidental. Label each one with its chromatic solfege syllable. Ask: is this raised (Di, Ri, Fi, Si, Li) or lowered (Ra, Me, Se, Le, Te)?
4
Sing slowly
Sing the melody at half tempo using solfege syllables. Take extra care approaching each chromatic pitch. Lean into the half-step motion: chromatic notes almost always resolve by step.
5
Perform at tempo
Once you have navigated the chromatic spots accurately, bring the tempo back up. Keep the key in your inner ear throughout.
⚠ WHERE STUDENTS GO WRONG
The most common mistake is guessing at a chromatic pitch instead of naming it first. Ear training research (and the OMT approach) is clear: name before you sing. If you cannot say the solfege syllable, you are not ready to sing the note. Slow down and label it.
✍️ PART 3 · MELODIC DICTATION WITH CHROMATICISM

Melodic dictation means writing down a melody you hear. When the melody is chromatic, the same solfege system protects you: once you identify a pitch's solfege syllable, you know exactly where to notate it.

Four-Step Dictation Protocol
A
Note the key. Listen for the key center on the first play-through. Identify Do before worrying about individual pitches.
B
Sing back on "la." On the second hearing, hum or sing the melody on a neutral syllable to lock in the contour and rhythm.
C
Identify scale degrees and chromatic alterations. Now name each pitch as a solfege syllable. Flag every chromatic note with its correct raised or lowered syllable.
D
Write in notation. Translate each solfege syllable to a notehead on staff paper (or MuseScore). Add accidentals as needed. Check: does the written version sound like what you heard?

Practice frequency matters more than session length. Short, focused dictation sessions (10-15 minutes daily) build inner hearing far faster than one long weekly session. At Levels 2 and 3 in OMT, you will encounter skips, some chromaticism, modulation passages, and more complex rhythms. All of these are handled by the same four-step protocol.

💡 INNER HEARING
The ultimate goal of all ear training is inner hearing: looking at written music and hearing it mentally before making any sound. Chromatic solfege is the map. The more syllables you know by reflex, the more accurately your inner ear can construct the sound from the page.

🤖 WORTH KNOWING · AI PITCH DETECTION AND SIGHT-SINGING PRACTICE
Pitch-detection apps can tell you accurately whether you sang the correct note. They cannot sight-read the phrase for you, and they cannot build the internal hearing, imagining a melody's sound before you make it, that sight-singing itself is meant to train. That skill has to be practiced through repetition, not automated.

💪 THE ONE THING TO REMEMBER

Chromatic solfege gives every pitch a name: raised pitches add "ee" (Di, Ri, Fi, Si, Li); lowered pitches change the vowel (Ra, Me, Se, Le, Te). Before you sing a chromatic melody, name every accidental with its solfege syllable. Name before you sing. That is the whole system.
