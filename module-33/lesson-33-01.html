🎵 TODAY'S FOCUS
Where have you grown this year, and where do you still want to go?
This module brings Theory II to a close. You have worked through minor scales and harmonization, SATB voice leading, modes, species counterpoint, seventh chords, modal composition, and formal analysis. Today you pull it all together: work through a curated set of capstone exercises from the OMT Workbook, evaluate your own performance honestly, and write a study plan that points toward what you want to learn next.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
Complete a curated set of OMT Workbook exercises spanning all seven Theory II content areas.
2
Self-assess your performance on each exercise type and identify specific areas for growth.
3
Create a personal study plan for continued music theory study beyond this course.

OMT Workbook, page 1108 (capstone exercises) or the digital version at viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory

📚 KEY VOCABULARY
Capstone Exercise
An exercise combining multiple skills from across the course into a single complex task. Capstone exercises require you to draw on everything you have learned, not just one isolated skill.
Self-Assessment
Evaluating your own work honestly against specified criteria; identifying both strengths and areas for improvement. Effective self-assessment is a skill composers and performers practice throughout their careers.
Personal Study Plan
A written plan for continuing to improve in music theory. A good study plan identifies specific goals, resources (chapters, recordings, exercises), and a realistic timeline. It makes growth intentional rather than accidental.
🎸 PART 1 · THE SEVEN CAPSTONE AREAS
Theory II Capstone Skills Wheel
Each spoke is one capstone area from the OMT Workbook. You will work on all seven in the capstone exercise set.

The OMT Workbook capstone section (p. 1108) gathers exercises from seven skill areas that represent the full scope of Theory II. You have studied each of these areas this year. A capstone exercise does not test just one skill in isolation. It asks you to combine skills: for example, harmonizing a minor melody requires you to apply your knowledge of minor scales, chord qualities, voice leading, and form at the same time.

# Capstone Area What the Exercise Asks
1 Minor Scale Harmonization Write chord symbols and Roman numerals for a given minor melody; choose between natural, harmonic, and melodic minor collections as appropriate.
2 SATB Voice Leading with Secondary Dominants Complete an SATB passage including at least one applied (secondary) dominant; follow all voice leading rules for parallel motion, doubling, and resolution.
3 Mode Identification Listen to or read three short melodies and identify the mode of each (Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian, or Locrian).
4 Species Counterpoint Write a first-, second-, or third-species counterpoint line above a given cantus firmus; check your work against consonance, dissonance, and motion rules.
5 Seventh Chord Voice Leading Resolve a series of seventh chords (V7, ii7, vii◦7) according to proper resolution conventions for the seventh and leading tone.
6 Modal Composition (Short Exercise) Compose 4-8 measures in a specified mode. Your melody should clearly establish the mode's characteristic scale degree through melodic emphasis and appropriate harmonization.
7 Form Identification Analyze a short piece and label its formal sections (binary, ternary, rounded binary, etc.); identify phrase beginnings, cadences, and any repetition or return.
💡 A NOTE ON THE DIGITAL WORKBOOK
The digital OMT platform at viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory offers interactive exercises with immediate scoring for most of these areas. If you want more practice on a weak area after completing the capstone set, the digital exercises are the best place to start. OMT is a living resource; check the site for updated content and new exercises that may not appear in your printed workbook.
🎯 PART 2 · SELF-ASSESSMENT CRITERIA

After completing each capstone exercise, evaluate your own work using four criteria. Be honest: self-assessment only helps you if it is accurate. Use the 1-4 scale below.

Self-Assessment Grid
After completing each exercise type, rate yourself 1-4 on each criterion and write a specific note about what you did well or what tripped you up. You will transfer these ratings to your personal study plan.

📋 PART 3 · YOUR PERSONAL STUDY PLAN

Your self-assessment ratings point directly toward a study plan. A plan that stays vague ("I want to get better at voice leading") does not produce results. A plan that is specific ("I will work through OMT Chapter 18 exercises 3-6 over the next two weeks, then check my work against the answer key, and listen to three Bach chorales looking for the same voice leading patterns") produces results.

✍️ YOUR PLAN MUST INCLUDE THESE FOUR THINGS
1
Two skills to strengthen, Choose from your two lowest self-assessment scores. Name the specific skill and why you rated it that way.
2
Specific OMT chapters to review, Name the chapter title and what you will do with it (read, do exercises, review examples).
3
Listening examples to study, Name at least one specific piece and one specific thing you will listen for.
4
What you want to learn next, Identify one topic beyond Theory II that interests you: jazz harmony, counterpoint, orchestration, film scoring, music technology, etc.
EXAMPLE, What a strong study plan looks like

Skills to strengthen: (1) Species counterpoint, I rated myself a 2 because I kept writing parallel octaves in second species. (2) Mode identification, I confuse Dorian and Aeolian when I am listening because they sound similar and I am not focusing on scale degree 6.

OMT chapters to review: Chapter 15 (Second Species), I will redo exercises 4 and 5. Chapter 4 (Modes), I will re-read the characteristic tone section and do the listening exercises at the end.

Listening examples: Purcell's Dido and Aeneas, Act I, I will listen for how the outer voices move in contrary motion in the counterpoint passages. Simon and Garfunkel's Scarborough Fair, I will listen for the Dorian raised 6th in the melody.

What I want to learn next: Jazz harmony. I want to understand ii-V-I progressions and how jazz musicians use seventh chord extensions (9ths, 11ths, 13ths) that go beyond what we covered in Theory II.

💡 REMEMBER
The goal of a study plan is not to be perfect now. It is to make your next steps clear so that improvement is a decision, not an accident. Music theory is a lifelong skill. Every professional musician continues to study it. The most important part of this capstone is not your score on any exercise, it is whether you walk away knowing exactly what to work on next and having a realistic plan to do it.

🤖 WORTH KNOWING · AI AND STUDY PLANNING
An AI tool can generate a generic music theory study plan in seconds, full of chapter names and practice suggestions. Only the student knows which specific skills from this course, whether that is species counterpoint, seventh chord resolution, or mode identification, still feel shaky after the capstone exercises. That honest self-assessment cannot be outsourced to a tool; it depends on the student's own accurate read of their own work.
WHAT COMES NEXT
Keep your personal study plan notes. They are a record of where you are now and where you want to grow next.
