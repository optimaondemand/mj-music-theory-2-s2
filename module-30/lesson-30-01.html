LESSON 30.01
Modal Composition: Dorian and Mixolydian
Composition Lesson
Part 1
Dorian and Mixolydian Modes: Side by Side

Dorian is natural minor with a raised ♯6. In D Dorian, that means B♮ instead of B♭. Build your progression around i and IV. The major IV chord (G major in D Dorian) is the characteristic chord that tells the listener "this is Dorian, not natural minor."

Mixolydian is major with a ♭7. In G Mixolydian, that means F♮ instead of F♯. Build your progression around I and ♭VII. The ♭VII chord (F major in G Mixolydian) is the characteristic chord that tells the listener "this is Mixolydian, not major."

Both modes avoid the leading tone. There is no V7-I pull. Modal music finds resolution through repetition and return to the tonic chord, not through tension and release.

Key Point

Modal music is everywhere: Dorian is common in jazz, Celtic, and folk music (think of "Scarborough Fair" or Radiohead). Mixolydian is all over rock and folk (think of "Norwegian Wood" or "Clocks"). You already know this sound.

Part 2
Pedal Point and Establishing the Modal Center

A pedal point is a sustained pitch , usually in the bass , held while harmonies move above it. In modal music, a pedal point on the tonic is powerful: it keeps anchoring your ear to the modal center without any V-I cadence. Even as the harmony moves from i to IV to ♭VII and back, the held bass D says "this is home." Use pedal points at the start and end of your composition to firmly establish the mode.

Part 3
Composer Statement Guide
What Your Composer Statement Must Answer
  • 1 Which mode did you choose (Dorian or Mixolydian) and why?
  • 2 Which OMT schema (p. 626) did you use as your harmonic framework? Name it (for example: "Dorian loop: i-IV-i-IV").
  • 3 What expressive quality does your modal choice give the piece? (Example: "Dorian gave my piece a melancholy but not defeated feeling because...")
  • 4 What specific harmonic or melodic choice did you make to establish the mode? (Example: "I used the major IV chord in measure 3 to mark the Dorian sound.")
Key Point

The composer statement is not a summary of what you did. It is an argument: explain why your modal choices create the specific sound and feeling you were going for.

🤖 WORTH KNOWING · AI AND MODAL COMPOSITION
AI composition tools can generate a complete piece in a chosen mode within seconds. This assignment asks for something different: the student's own composing, performing or exporting the piece as audio, and a written self-critique measured against the assignment's own criteria. That self-evaluation, judging whether the mode actually comes through in a piece you made, is not something a generator can do in a student's place.

  2. Modal Concept Check (short Canvas quiz, 5 questions): A quick check on Dorian, Mixolydian, and pedal point , to confirm the theory before you submit your composition.
Wrap-Up

Dorian (raised ♯6, characteristic major IV) and Mixolydian (♭7, characteristic ♭VII) are two modes you can use for composition right now with the tools you already have. Neither uses V-I pull as its primary harmonic engine. Instead, repeat your tonic, feature your characteristic chord, and use a pedal point to lock in the modal center. Your composer statement explains why every choice you made serves the mode's expressive quality.
